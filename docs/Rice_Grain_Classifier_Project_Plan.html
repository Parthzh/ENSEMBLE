1   What Are We Building?

We are building a smart app that can look at a photo of a rice grain and tell you:

  1. What variety of rice it is (Arborio, Basmati, Ipsala, Jasmine, or Karacadag)
  2. How confident it is about its answer (e.g., "96% sure it's Basmati")
  3. Physical details about the grain — its size, shape, color, and texture

Think of it like Shazam, but for rice. You upload a photo, the app analyzes it, and gives you a full report.

Why Does This Matter?

Right now, rice grading is done by hand — people sit and visually inspect thousands of grains. This is slow, inconsistent (different people may grade the same grain differently), and expensive. Our app does the same job in seconds, and it never gets tired or inconsistent. This has real-world value in the food and agriculture industry.

3   The 4 Parameters We Analyze

When we analyze a rice grain, we look at 4 physical characteristics:

1. Size — How big is the grain?

What we measure: Length, Width, Area, Perimeter. Measured in pixels.
Why it matters: Basmati rice is long, while Arborio is short and fat.

2. Shape — What's the grain's overall shape?

L/W RatioShape LabelExample Variety
More than 3.0Long SlenderBasmati
2.1 to 3.0MediumJasmine
1.1 to 2.0BoldArborio
1.0 or lessRoundKaracadag

We also measure circularity and solidity.

3. Color — What color is the grain?

Average RGB values from the masked grain region. Labels: White, Creamy, Tan, or Brown.

4. Texture — How smooth or rough is the surface?

We apply a Laplacian filter (edge-detection) to the grain surface and calculate the standard deviation.

Texture Std DevLabelInterpretation
Greater than 10RoughMore surface variation
10 or lessSmoothUniform surface — polished rice
New: Texture is our 4th parameter. The original plan covered only 3. Texture analysis uses the Laplacian operator from OpenCV — a well-established method for measuring surface variation.

Why Pixels and Not Millimeters?

Every image is 250 × 250 pixels, but we don't know the camera zoom. For classification, what matters is the ratio between length and width, not the actual mm value.

How It Works (7 Steps — OpenCV Pipeline)

  1. Input — User uploads a rice grain photo or selects a dataset sample
  2. Grayscale — Convert to single channel for edge detection
  3. Gaussian Blur — Reduce noise with 5×5 kernel
  4. Otsu Thresholding — Separate grain from background automatically
  5. Contour Detection — Find the grain outline
  6. Ellipse Fitting — Fit ellipse + bounding rectangle for size/shape
  7. Texture Analysis — Compute Laplacian standard deviation
Current Status: All 7 pipeline steps are implemented and working. Each step's visual output is displayed to the user in real time.

Group A — Deep Learning (Images)

ModelWhat It IsTarget
Custom CNN3 conv blocks, from scratch~95–97%
MobileNetV2Transfer learning, frozen base~99%

See raw pixels. Learn features automatically. Do NOT use the OpenCV pipeline.

Group B — Classical ML (Numbers)

ModelHow It Works
SVMMathematical boundaries between types
Random Forest100+ decision trees, majority vote
KNNFinds most similar grains

Use 108 extracted numbers (size + shape + color + texture), not images.

Why build both? We compare them and answer: "Is deep learning actually better than simpler methods for rice classification?" This comparison is our paper's core contribution.
